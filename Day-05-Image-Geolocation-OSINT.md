## 1. Introduction

On Day 5 of my cybersecurity training, I learned how to use publicly available information and visual clues to investigate images.

Image geolocation is the process of estimating where a photograph was taken by examining details such as buildings, road signs, landmarks, and the surrounding environment.

I also learned how OSINT techniques can help investigators collect and verify information from public sources.

## 2. Image Geolocation

Image geolocation helps identify the possible location where an image was captured.

### Techniques Learned

- **Landmark identification:** Recognizing well-known buildings, monuments, and landmarks.
- **Road signs:** Examining road names, traffic signs, and other visible clues.
- **Environmental clues:** Observing buildings, roads, vegetation, and the surrounding area.
- **Reverse image search:** Finding visually similar images and possible original sources.
- **Location verification:** Comparing clues with reliable public sources before drawing conclusions.

## 3. Tools and Resources

### A. Google Maps

Google Maps can help compare visible landmarks, roads, and buildings with real-world locations.

**Website:** https://maps.google.com/

**Practical steps:**
1. Observe the visible clues in an image.
2. Search for possible locations using relevant keywords.
3. Compare buildings, roads, and landmarks.
4. Check whether multiple clues support the same location.

### B. Google Lens

Google Lens can help find visually similar images and identify objects or landmarks.

**Website:** https://lens.google.com/

**Practical steps:**
1. Open Google Lens.
2. Upload an image you are permitted to analyse.
3. Review visually similar images and relevant search results.
4. Compare the results with the clues visible in the original image.

### C. Google Search

Google Search can help find publicly available information about landmarks, buildings, and locations.

**Example search:**
```text
famous clock tower in London
```

The search query can be changed according to the clues found in the image.

## 4. Missing Person OSINT Activity

I also learned about the use of OSINT techniques in missing-person investigations.

Publicly available photographs, news reports, official announcements, and other reliable sources may provide useful clues during an investigation.

Important considerations:
- Verify information using reliable sources.
- Do not treat an unverified online claim as a confirmed fact.
- Respect personal privacy.
- Share relevant information with the appropriate authorities rather than publicly accusing or identifying people based on guesses.
