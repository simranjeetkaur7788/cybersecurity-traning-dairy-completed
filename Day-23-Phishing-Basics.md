## 1. Introduction

On this day, I learned about phishing, which is one of the most common cyber security threats. Phishing is a type of cyberattack in which attackers try to trick people into sharing sensitive information, such as passwords, banking details, OTPs, or personal information.

Attackers often pretend to be trusted organizations, banks, companies, or government departments. They may contact victims through emails, text messages, phone calls, or fake websites.

The main purpose of phishing is to steal information, spread malware, or gain unauthorized access to a computer system or online account.

## 2. What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering technique that uses fake messages or websites to deceive users. Attackers create messages that appear genuine and encourage people to click on suspicious links, open harmful attachments, or enter their personal details.

**Example:** A person receives an email claiming that their bank account will be blocked unless they verify their account immediately. The email contains a link to a fake login page that steals their credentials.

## 3. Types of Phishing

### A. Email Phishing
Attackers send fake emails that appear to come from trusted companies or organizations. These emails may contain suspicious links or harmful attachments.

### B. Spear Phishing
Spear phishing targets a specific person or organization. The attacker may use personal or professional information to make the message more believable.

### C. Smishing
Smishing is phishing carried out through SMS or text messages. For example, a person may receive a fake message about a parcel delivery or bank account problem.

### D. Vishing
Vishing is phishing through voice calls. Attackers may pretend to be bank employees, technical support staff, or government officials to obtain confidential information.

### E. Whaling
Whaling is a type of targeted phishing attack aimed at senior executives or other important individuals in an organization.

### F. Website Phishing
Attackers create fake websites that look similar to legitimate websites. When users enter their login details, the information may be stolen.

## 4. Social Engineering Principles

Social engineering involves manipulating people into taking actions that may compromise their security. Phishing attacks commonly use the following techniques:

- **Urgency:** Creating pressure to act quickly.
- **Fear:** Threatening account suspension or financial loss.
- **Authority:** Pretending to be a manager, bank official, or government employee.
- **Trust:** Impersonating a familiar person or organization.
- **Curiosity:** Using interesting or surprising messages to encourage clicks.
- **Scarcity:** Claiming that an offer is available for a limited time.

These techniques influence people to act without checking whether a message is genuine.

## 5. Common Signs of a Phishing Attack

During this topic, I learned that suspicious messages can be identified by checking the following signs:

1. The sender's email address does not match the official organization.
2. The message contains spelling or grammatical mistakes.
3. It creates unnecessary urgency or threatens account closure.
4. It asks for passwords, OTPs, or banking details.
5. It contains suspicious links or shortened URLs.
6. It includes unexpected attachments.
7. It offers prizes, rewards, or deals that appear too good to be true.
8. The website address contains spelling mistakes or an unusual domain name.

However, not every phishing message contains these signs. Some attacks are carefully designed to look genuine, so the sender and website should always be verified.

## 6. How Phishing Attacks Work

A phishing attack generally follows these steps:

1. **Preparation:** The attacker chooses a target and creates a deceptive message.
2. **Delivery:** The message is sent through email, SMS, social media, or a phone call.
3. **Deception:** The message encourages the victim to trust it or act quickly.
4. **User Interaction:** The victim clicks a link, opens an attachment, or provides information.
5. **Attack Outcome:** The attacker may obtain credentials, steal information, or cause malware to run.
6. **Misuse:** The stolen information may be used for unauthorized access or fraud.

Understanding these steps helps security professionals recognize and prevent phishing attacks.

## 7. Prevention of Phishing Attacks

The following safety measures can reduce the risk of phishing:

- Verify the sender before trusting a message.
- Check website addresses carefully before entering login details.
- Never share passwords or OTPs through suspicious messages or calls.
- Avoid opening unexpected attachments.
- Use multi-factor authentication (MFA) wherever possible.
- Keep the operating system, browser, and security software updated.
- Report suspicious emails or messages to the appropriate security team.
- Use strong, unique passwords for different accounts.
- Access important services through their official websites or applications rather than suspicious links.
