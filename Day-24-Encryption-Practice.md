# Day 24: Encryption Practice

**Date:** 23 July 2026  
**Topic:** Practical Understanding of Encryption

## 1. Introduction
On this day, I practised the basic concepts of encryption and learned how encryption helps protect confidential information. Encryption converts readable information, called plaintext, into an unreadable form called ciphertext using an encryption algorithm and a key.

## 2. Working of Encryption
Encryption generally involves three main components:
- **Plaintext:** The original readable message.
- **Encryption algorithm:** The method used to transform the message.
- **Key:** Information used by the algorithm to encrypt the data.

The encrypted message can be converted back into readable form through decryption when the correct key and method are available.

## 3. Types of Encryption
**Symmetric encryption:** The same secret key is used for encryption and decryption. It is generally efficient for protecting large amounts of data.

**Asymmetric encryption:** A public key and a private key are used for related cryptographic operations. It supports techniques such as public-key encryption and digital signatures.

## 4. Practical Learning
I practised converting simple messages into encrypted form and reviewed how the original message could be recovered through decryption. I also learned that encryption and hashing are different: encryption is designed to be reversible with the appropriate key, while cryptographic hashing produces a fixed-length digest and is not normally reversible.

## 5. Applications
Encryption is used in secure messaging, online banking, HTTPS connections, file protection, and secure data storage.

## 6. Conclusion
This practice helped me understand the basic encryption process and the importance of protecting keys. I learned that correct key management is essential for maintaining the confidentiality of sensitive information.

## 7. Learning Platforms and References
- Python documentation for learning programming concepts.
- Cybersecurity learning materials about encryption and decryption.

# Day 25: Decryption Practice

**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**Topic:** Understanding Decryption

## 1. Introduction
On this day, I practised the concept of decryption. Decryption is the process of converting encrypted data, known as ciphertext, back into its original readable form, known as plaintext.

## 2. How Decryption Works
Decryption uses a suitable algorithm and the required cryptographic key to recover the original information. In symmetric encryption, the same secret key is used for encryption and decryption. In asymmetric cryptography, the relevant key depends on the operation being performed.

## 3. Important Terms
- **Plaintext:** Original readable information.
- **Ciphertext:** Information after encryption.
- **Encryption key:** Key used in the encryption process.
- **Decryption key:** Key required to recover the plaintext.
- **Algorithm:** A defined method for performing a cryptographic operation.

## 4. Practical Learning
I reviewed simple encryption and decryption examples and observed how the correct key allows encrypted information to be recovered. I also learned that an incorrect key or an incorrect algorithm may prevent successful decryption.

For safe practice, cryptographic exercises should use sample messages and locally created test files rather than sensitive personal information.

## 5. Importance of Decryption
Decryption allows authorized users to access protected files, read secure communications, and recover information stored in encrypted form.

## 6. Security Considerations
Encryption keys should be stored securely and shared only through appropriate methods. Strong, well-established cryptographic algorithms should be used instead of relying on simple substitution methods for real-world protection.

## 7. Conclusion
This practical session improved my understanding of how encrypted data is recovered. I learned that encryption and decryption work together to protect information while allowing authorized access.
